Evacuation Plans at PMU Québec

How I design and produce clear evacuation plans in my role as a project manager at PMU Québec. The plans shown on this page are fictional examples, created for demonstration only.

01

My Role at PMU Québec

An evacuation plan has to be read in seconds, by someone who has never been inside the building.

I work as an evacuation plan project manager at PMU Québec, a Québec fire-prevention company. It is a salaried position: I follow up on projects, draw the plans from the documents provided and take them all the way to print files. These projects belong to PMU Québec and its clients. They are not DomDesign projects.

02

My Contribution to Plan Design and Production

On every plan, my work covers five areas.

  • Visual hierarchy: I bring the You Are Here marker, routes and exits forward first, and keep building details in grey, in the background.
  • Plan legibility: I set text sizes, line weights and spacing for the format actually posted on the wall, not just for the screen.
  • Signage: I apply the same legend and symbols from one plan to the next, so information is always found the same way.
  • Technical drawing: I draw walls, doors with their swing direction, corridors and stairs in AutoCAD from the documents provided.
  • Print preparation: I check formats, margins, colours and fonts, then prepare files that are ready to print and install.

My ground rule: every graphic choice should help someone find the exit faster. If an element does not help the reading, it moves to the background.

03

Fictional Examples That Show My Approach

I do not publish any plan made for a client. To show how I work, I drew two demonstration scenarios from scratch: Centre civique des Ormes and Pavillon communautaire des Aulnes. These buildings do not exist. Their geometry, rooms and access points are invented and do not reuse any plan, address or logo from a real project.
