Concept Case Study · Sourced via Guru.com

R.E.N.O.I.R. — Legacy Collection

Apparel graphic design and AI-assisted mockup visualization for an independent streetwear brand's debut "Legacy" collection — T-shirt, jacket, and bag pieces built for an engagement that ended without a client response after concepts were delivered.

01

Overview & my role

An independent streetwear brand needed a launch-ready visual identity across a small apparel range — before any of it existed as a physical sample.

R.E.N.O.I.R. posted a design request on Guru.com for its debut "Legacy" collection — a "luxury minimalist streetwear" positioning built around a tone-on-tone wordmark and the line "Not Just a Name — A Legacy." Unlike Bark's multi-designer lead model, Guru works closer to a written proposal: scope and price agreed before creative work starts. I proposed a fixed-fee engagement covering graphic direction for a T-shirt, a bomber jacket, and a bag, plus premium mockup visualization for each — built in Kittl.com — so the brand would have real launch assets before committing to a sample run or a photoshoot.

  • Design the wordmark lockup and back-panel tagline treatment for the T-shirt, in both a high-contrast and a tonal (black-on-black) colorway.
  • Extend the same graphic language to a bomber jacket and a bag concept.
  • Build photorealistic-style mockups for each piece in place of a physical sample shoot.
  • Scope and price the work as a fixed-fee proposal through Guru's bid system.

02

Project phases

  1. Phase 1

    Brief & Proposal Won bid

    Guru.com proposal

    Responded to the client's Guru listing with a fixed-fee proposal for wordmark and back-graphic design across three pieces — T-shirt, jacket, and bag — plus mockup visualization for each. The client accepted the proposal and shared reference direction for a "luxury minimalist streetwear" mood.

  2. Phase 2

    T-Shirt Graphics & Mockups Delivered

    Core deliverable

    Front wordmark lockup and back-panel tagline ("Not Just a Name — A Legacy") in two colorways — a high-contrast white-on-black version and a tonal black-on-black version — each rendered as a full mockup set built in Kittl.com.

  3. Phase 3

    Jacket & Bag Concepts Delivered

    Extended scope

    The same graphic language extended to a bomber jacket and a bag concept, rendered as mockups in the same visual system, rounding out the collection's first proposed range.

  4. Phase 4

    Client Follow-Up No response

    Engagement status

    All concepts were delivered on schedule. The client did not respond to follow-up messages after delivery — no revisions were requested and no next phase was confirmed. The engagement is currently closed with no further activity from the client's side.

03

What the client asked for

  • A "luxury minimalist streetwear" positioning — restrained, premium, no loud graphics.
  • A wordmark and tagline system that could carry across multiple garment types, not a single hero print.
  • Launch-ready mockups convincing enough to use for pre-order marketing, with no sample run yet in production.
  • Two distinct colorway directions to choose between for the debut drop.

The key difficulty: a genuinely minimalist brief leaves almost nowhere to hide — with no loud print or logo to lean on, the wordmark lockup, its exact placement, and the mockup's lighting and fabric rendering had to do all the work of reading as "premium" rather than "blank T-shirt with text."

04

T-shirt graphics & mockups

The delivered T-shirt visuals, in both colorway directions — a high-contrast lockup shot on location, and a tonal black-on-black version photographed against concrete and graffiti backdrops. The jacket and bag concepts followed the same system but aren't shown here.

A note on these images: every visual on this page is an AI-assisted mockup built in Kittl.com, not product photography — no physical sample was sewn or shot. That was the point of the deliverable: a convincing pre-launch visual the brand could use before committing to a production run.

05

What worked

  • The wordmark lockup translated cleanly across both colorways and both garment orientations (front/back) without needing to be redrawn.
  • Two distinct colorway directions gave the client a real choice rather than a single take-it-or-leave-it concept.
  • Extending the same graphic system to the jacket and bag kept the collection feeling like one line instead of three unrelated pieces.
  • Delivering on schedule meant the stalled follow-up was entirely on the client's side, not a production delay on mine.
06

Challenges & lessons

  • Guru's proposal model meant pricing and scoping three garment types before seeing how the brief would actually resolve visually — a different risk than the single-deliverable Bark or HoneyBook engagements.
  • A minimalist brief has a low margin for error: small placement or scale choices on the wordmark were the difference between "premium" and "generic blank tee."
  • The engagement going quiet after delivery, with no revision requests and no close-out message, is the clearest lesson here — I now build a scheduled follow-up message into every fixed-fee proposal, so a stalled client relationship has a defined last step rather than trailing off indefinitely.
  • Without a signed-off next phase, the jacket and bag concepts remain unshown publicly beyond this write-up — a reminder that "delivered" and "confirmed" are two different milestones, same as with any concept engagement.
07

Current status

All contracted concepts — T-shirt (two colorways), jacket, and bag — were delivered on schedule. The client has not responded since delivery:

  • T-shirt graphics & mockups — delivered, shown above.
  • Jacket & bag concepts — delivered, not shown publicly pending any future client sign-off.
  • Production files, colorway selection, and any sample run — never reached, engagement inactive since delivery.
